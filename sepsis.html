Our cause

For Susan.

Sue’s Angels FC was founded in memory of Susan Anne Martin, who we lost to sepsis. We play in her name, and we share what we have learned so other families can recognise the signs in time.

01 Understanding sepsis

What sepsis is

Sepsis is what can happen when the body’s response to an infection starts to harm its own tissues and organs. It can affect anyone, at any age, and it can turn serious very quickly. That is why noticing it early matters so much.

The UK Sepsis Trust estimates that sepsis takes around 48,000 lives in the UK every year. Many of those losses could have been prevented, and the biggest difference is spotting it early. That is why we keep talking about it.

02 Know the signs

Could it be sepsis?

In an adult, trust your instinct and get help quickly. Call 999 or NHS 111, and ask the question if you notice any of these.

S

Slurred speech or confusion

Sudden disorientation, drowsiness or trouble speaking clearly.

E

Extreme shivering or muscle pain

Shaking, fever, or pain that feels far worse than usual.

P

Passing no urine in a day

A clear drop in how often they go to the toilet.

S

Severe breathlessness

Struggling for breath, or breathing very fast.

I

“It feels like I’m going to die”

A deep, sudden sense that something is seriously wrong.

S

Skin mottled, bluish or pale

Blotchy, discoloured or unusually pale skin.

In a child or baby

Call 999 or go straight to A&E if a child:

  • Is breathing very fast
  • Has a fit or convulsion
  • Looks mottled, bluish or pale
  • Has a rash that doesn’t fade when you press it
  • Is very lethargic or hard to wake
  • Feels abnormally cold to touch

For babies under 5, also look out for not feeding, repeated vomiting, or no wet nappy for 12 hours.

Trust your instinct

If someone is getting worse quickly, please do not wait. Ask the question: could it be sepsis?

This page shares general awareness information, not medical advice. For full, up to date guidance please visit the UK Sepsis Trust and the NHS.

03 Get involved

Three ways to help

01

Know and share the signs

The signs above can save a life. Take a moment to learn them, and share them with the people you love.

02

Support the UK Sepsis Trust

They support families, fund research and raise awareness across the country. You can find out more or give on their website.

Stand with the club
