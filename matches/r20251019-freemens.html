  3. Sue's Angels FC 7-2 Old Freemen's
League Ten Won

Sun, 19 October 2025 · 10:30

Sue’s Angels FC
7–2
Old Freemen's

Meadhurst Sports Clun

01 Match report

How it went

Sue's Angels' perfect league record remained intact with a commanding 7-2 victory over Old Freemen's at Meadhurst Sports Club, extending the club's unbeaten run to five wins from five and moving the goal tally to an impressive 28 from their opening five matches. The scoreline tells the story of a team in complete control—dominant, fluid, and finding the net with relentless efficiency. Old Freemen's, despite managing two goals, never posed a serious threat to Sue's Angels' ambitions this season, and the morning's work was a statement of intent from a squad moving through the division with real purpose.

The XI took to the pitch in their familiar 4-2-3-1 shape: Luke Munns between the sticks, Andrew Allen at left-back, Michael Brabrook and Daniel McLane as the centre-back pairing, and Dean Knight on the right flank. Richard Epathite and Stewart Luwawa anchored the midfield, while Stephen Potter, Frazier-Isaías Osunkoya, and Malachi Mullings operated behind Charlie Dunkley in attack. The bench held reinforcements in Jeev Thilaganathan, William Clark, and Abiola Adio. From the first whistle, Sue's Angels looked dangerous throughout the match with constant attacking pressure, and Old Freemen's found themselves under immediate siege.

The goals came with impressive regularity. Frazier-Isaías Osunkoya proved unstoppable, finding the net twice and establishing himself as a central threat in the attacking third. The finishing was clinical when it mattered, with Andrew Allen pulling the strings from left-back and earning two assists before Charlie Dunkley also chipped in with two of his own to fuel the attack. Dean Knight added his name to the scoresheet, profiting from the numerical advantage in the final third, while William Clark made a decisive impact from the bench as a centre-forward, adding another goal to the tally. Daniel McLane, the centre-back, also got among the scorers via a set piece—a reminder of Sue's Angels' threat from dead-ball situations. Stephen Potter contributed a goal of his own amid an otherwise strong team performance that maintained momentum through an impressive opening stretch to the season. Malachi Mullings orchestrated play from his position in midfield, providing crucial assists that underpinned the attacking flow.

What made the afternoon particularly notable was Old Freemen's finally breaching Sue's Angels' previously impenetrable defence. Over the first four league matches, the club had kept four clean sheets, a record that had built a protective wall around the season's early narrative. Old Freemen's forward celebrated wildly when his goal went in—a rare moment of joy for the away side, born partly from the rarity of finding the back of the net against this Sue's Angels defence. The second Old Freemen's goal was an unfortunate own goal from Stephen Potter late in the game, a moment of misfortune rather than any systemic collapse. By that stage, the result was long secured, and such incidents barely registered against the backdrop of Sue's Angels' dominance.

Throughout the match, the boys continued to show their attacking quality and intensity going forward, moving the ball with purpose and creating opportunities with alarming regularity. The attacking pressure never relented, and Old Freemen's never found a comfortable rhythm or a way to genuinely trouble Munns in goal. When substitutions came—Brabrook and Knight withdrawn, Thilaganathan moving to centre-back, Clark introduced at centre-forward, and Adio adding fresh legs on the wing—the pattern of play remained the same. Sue's Angels controlled the game from start to finish and dominated large periods throughout, the sort of performance that justifies a perfect record and speaks to genuine quality within the squad.

Five games, five wins, 28 goals scored, just two conceded. Those numbers don't lie. Sue's Angels have announced themselves as serious contenders in Division Ten, and performances like this—fluid, clinical, and marked by a relentless attacking intent—suggest they'll only tighten their grip as the season deepens. Old Freemen's will have learned a hard lesson about the level required to compete at the top of this division. For Sue's Angels, it's another statement, another three points, another step towards where the ambition of this club is clearly pointing.
