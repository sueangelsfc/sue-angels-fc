03The report Written by the club

The full report.

Angels hit six to make it five from five

Back at The Reeves, back among the goals. Our first league game since September finished 6–1 against TSM Rovers: five wins from five, and three points clear at the top.

Leon Burnett, Charlie Dunkley and Ade Owolana had it 3–1 at the break. The bench finished it. Frazier-Isaías Osunkoya made it four, and Samakab Nur came on at right wing-back and scored twice.

A week on from going out of the Marcus Lipton Cup on penalties at Juizo, this was the response the gaffer wanted. Five games, 15 points, 26 scored, four conceded.

Finding a way through a compact five

TSM came with a plan and stuck to it: 4–5–1, everybody behind the ball, no interest in being pulled out of shape. The only way through a side set up like that is side to side until something opens.

We thought Charlie had opened it inside five minutes, but the flag went up. It looked a close call, him level with the last man or behind him as the ball was played, and we did not have to wait much longer.

The gaffer said afterwards that the chalked-off goal was the first sign of a different team in front of goal to the one that could not find a way through a week earlier.

The opener came on 16 minutes, from a corner they could not clear. Dean Knight’s delivery found Daniel McLane at the back post and the effort was blocked. Andrew Allen won the loose ball near the touchline and worked it back inside to Stewart Luwawa, who took it down on his chest and went for the volley. He did not catch it, and it dropped kindly for LJ, who swivelled and struck it right-footed across the goalkeeper. 1–0.

Eight minutes later, two. Jim El Bayati slid Charlie in on the right of the box and he lifted the finish over the goalkeeper without breaking stride.

Their goalkeeper keeps them in it

Our best football came down the left, where Michael Brabrook, Andy and Ade kept getting TSM into twos and threes. One move ended with Ade weighting a cross onto Adam Marie’s head, and the header went straight at the goalkeeper. A yard either side of him and it is three.

More chances came, and he answered every one of them. Those saves kept TSM in the match, and on 33 minutes they had their reward.

There was not much anybody could do about it. A free kick from distance flew into the top-right corner, Luke Munns beaten the moment it left the boot. A postage-stamp finish, and something for the visitors to hold on to.

Ade restores the cushion

We kept going at them from set pieces, Ade and Dean taking the corners from either side and TSM struggling with everything that came into the box.

The third, on 41 minutes, came from one of Mike’s long throws. It caused the trouble those throws usually cause, ricocheting around the area until a defender hacked it clear, and the clearance ran straight back out to Mike on the touchline. He crossed first time, LJ got across his marker and laid it off, and Ade met it with a left-footed volley. 3–1.

A good goal at a good time. TSM had just found a way back into the match, and we went in two clear with three finishes of real quality behind us.

Dan’s reading of the first half was that the patience was the point: keep it at the back, do not force it, go through the gaps when they appear.

The bench makes its mark

Mike spent the afternoon in a private contest with their goalkeeper. He got himself into position again and again, and every time the keeper had an answer.

On the hour the gaffer made a triple change: Sebastian Morally, Frazier and Jon Lloyd on for Jimi, Charlie and LJ. Sama replaced Dean at right wing-back on 70 minutes, and from there the substitutes took the game over.

The fourth came on 73. Stewart played the ball through, a moment of hesitation between centre-back and goalkeeper was all Frazier needed, and he nipped between the two of them, rounded the keeper and finished with his left foot, the covering defender on the line with no chance of reaching it.

Jon made the fifth. He had been a problem for their left-back since coming on, threatening to go either side of him; this time he went outside, reached the byline and drove into the box before cutting it back for Sama to finish from close range on 77.

Four minutes later Sama was there again, and the sixth started in our own box. A TSM forward got his shot away, it was tame enough to go straight into Luke’s arms, and Luke threw it wide to Mike with the left flank to himself. Two touches and it was down the line to Liam Kirby, barely a minute on the pitch, who took it down and slid a pass into Frazier’s path. The cross was first time, and Sama opened his body to place it in. Six.

He nearly had the hat-trick as well, and their goalkeeper denied him with a save as good as any he made all afternoon. With the match long gone he was still making us work, and he deserves the credit for it.

Two goals and almost a third after coming on at right wing-back is some contribution, and the runs into the box were what did it. He talked us through both finishes afterwards, and which one he is claiming.

Twenty-three league wins in a row now, back through last season’s unbeaten title. There is a lot of football still to play, but five from five is exactly the start we wanted in League Eight.

What they said afterwards

Three interviews from The Reeves, each with the recording:

  • Sama on two goals from right wing-back, after coming on in the 70th minute and taking Man of the Match.
  • Dan on patience, width and a changing room he enjoys, on what opened TSM up and the group he captains.
  • The gaffer on a different team in front of goal, on the response to last week and the mood at the final whistle.

Next up, first meets second

We go to Clapham Common on Sunday 8 November to face Brockwell Violets, kick-off 10:30am. They sit three points behind us after a 1–0 defeat to Three Little Birds, which gives the meeting a little extra edge.

Last season we finished champions and Brockwell runners-up. Now we meet again in League Eight with both clubs at the top. A win puts us six points clear of them; a win for Brockwell brings them level.

Still early days, but this is one to look forward to.

Thank you to TSM Rovers for the game. We wish them all the best for the rest of the season.
