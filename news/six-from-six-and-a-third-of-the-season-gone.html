Six from six, and a third of the season gone

11 November 2026 · 4 min read · Sue's Angels FC

News Six from six, and a third of the season gone 11 Nov 2026Sue’s Angels FC

Eighteen league matches in League Ten last season, six in League Eight this one, and no league defeat in any of them. Six games is a third of this season, which makes it a fair point to stop and look at what we have actually done.

Brockwell Violets have played the same six and are six points back. The table stands at 8 November; the full division is on the league page.

The sixth one was the statement

The last of the six was first against second: Brockwell Violets at The Reeves, both sides unbeaten, 6-1, and most of the second half played with ten men after a second yellow for Luke Munns.

It was a statement. We're top.

"I thought in the first half we were relentless in terms of the chances that we created and the possession with the ball in their half," said Stephen Epathite afterwards. "We were on the front foot from minute one."

On going down to ten: "I think we've got a lot of older heads, a lot of sensible guys in here that know how to manage matches. They managed the game really well. They managed to score another goal. The game management was brilliant."

And on what the result says: "We've won 6-1. I think that if we had finished with eleven it could have been double, and I think we were in that mood. It sends out a message that we are still strong." The full interview is on the site, as is the match report.

Six goals the week before, and a cup exit before that

A week earlier it was 6-1 at home to TSM Rovers, seven days after going out of the Marcus Lipton Cup on penalties at Juizo without scoring in ninety minutes.

We looked like a different team to last week in front of goal.

"We're more composed. We look more alive in the final third. We look hungry," the manager said of the change. "We had more desire to score a goal this week, and that's proved with the six goals."

The part he kept coming back to was the dressing room. "The camaraderie and the spirit in the players after the game, I thought that was the best part. It felt like last season when it was a feel-good: we've come off the pitch and no one's heads down or annoyed."

Who has done it

12Goals, Ade Owolana
14Goals and assists, Charlie Dunkley
7Assists, Dean Knight

Ade Owolana has his twelve in eight matches. The division's next-best scorers, Victor Awosanya of Three Little Birds and Vincent Narayna of TSM Rovers, have six each.

Charlie Dunkley has had a hand in fourteen goals, Leon Burnett in nine, and Dean Knight has made seven and scored one, which is a season of a different shape and the one most easily missed.

Twenty-four players have been used in the eight competitive matches, and three of those eight were clean sheets.

What a third of a season can say, and what it cannot

We are scoring 5.3 a game against 6.0 in League Ten last year, and conceding 0.8 against 0.7. Those are close enough together that six matches cannot tell us whether this is a better side than last season's, four divisions down. Saying so now is better than producing a chart in May that looks like an answer.

One thing the record does support, because it rests on all 38 matches played out across the two seasons rather than on six:

  • We score just over five a game at The Reeves and just under four away from it.
  • We concede the same amount in both.
  • It holds separately in each season, which is what makes it worth watching rather than a quirk of a good month.

The cups

Out of the Supreme Trophies Marcus Lipton Cup, beaten 4-3 on penalties at Juizo after a goalless ninety minutes on 25 October. Still in the Brian Howard President's Cup, after winning 4-0 at Peps All Stars. And into the Surrey FA Sunday Lower Junior County Cup, which starts this Sunday.

Three in a row at The Reeves

The Station in the county cup on 15 November, Brockwell Violets in the league on the 22nd, and Celtic Swans in the President's Cup on the 29th. All three at home, all three at 11:00. The fixtures page has the rest.
