02The report Written by the club

The full report.

Sue’s Angels FC began their League Eight campaign with a hard-fought 2–1 victory over Three Little Birds FC at The Reeves Sports Club.

Following last season’s perfect league campaign and a two-division rise, the Angels entered the new season with high ambitions. However, their opening fixture quickly demonstrated that points at this level would have to be earned.

Three Little Birds started the game strongly and caused several problems during the opening exchanges. The visitors played with confidence, moved the ball effectively and created opportunities that forced Sue’s Angels onto the back foot.

Luke Munns was called into action on several occasions and produced a number of important saves to keep the game level. His eight saves across the match proved vital, particularly during a difficult opening period in which the Angels struggled to find their usual rhythm.

Sue’s Angels’ task was made more difficult when Andy Allen pulled up with a hamstring injury. Stephen Potter was introduced at left-back, while the team moved away from its starting 4-1-3-2 formation and switched to a 4-2-3-1.

The tactical adjustment made an immediate difference.

The additional presence in midfield gave the Angels greater control, improved the team’s defensive balance and gradually shifted the momentum of the game. After weathering the early pressure, Sue’s Angels began to play with more confidence and find spaces in the opposition half.

The breakthrough arrived following excellent hold-up play from Leon Burnett. Leon used his strength to bring the ball under control and helped release Charlie Dunkley into a dangerous position inside the penalty area.

Rather than forcing an attempt at goal, Charlie showed composure and awareness to lay the ball into the path of Jon Lloyd. Lloyd made no mistake, producing a classy finish to put the Angels ahead.

The opening goal changed the feeling around the game. Sue’s Angels began playing with greater belief, and just five minutes later they doubled their advantage through an extraordinary moment from Dunkley.

Collecting the ball around 30 yards from goal, Charlie unleashed a stunning right-footed strike seemingly out of nowhere. The ball flew beyond the Three Little Birds goalkeeper and into the net, leaving him with absolutely no chance.

It was a spectacular finish and an immediate contender for the club’s Goal of the Season award.

Having endured a challenging start, the Angels had shown their quality to establish a two-goal advantage. The players protected the lead for the remainder of the first half and went into the interval 2–0 ahead.

The half-time message was clear.

The players were reminded of the standards they had established and the achievements that had brought them to League Eight. They were also reminded of the level of concentration required to finish the job. There was a clear focus on limiting the opposition’s opportunities during the second half and managing the game more effectively.

For much of the second period, the contest resembled the calm before the storm. The game passed without any major incident, with Sue’s Angels managing their advantage while Three Little Birds searched for a way back into the match.

However, with 11 minutes remaining, the visitors were handed renewed hope.

Munns, who had been dependable throughout the contest and had played such an important role in keeping the Angels ahead, misjudged a corner that went directly into the goal. It was an unfortunate error from a goalkeeper who had otherwise produced several crucial saves.

The goal immediately changed the atmosphere.

At 2–1, Three Little Birds sensed an opportunity to rescue a point on the opening day and increased the pressure during the closing stages. Sue’s Angels were required to demonstrate a different side of their game.

In response, the Angels made another tactical adjustment and moved into a 5-3-2 formation. The change provided greater defensive security, added another presence across the back line and allowed the team to remain compact through midfield.

The players stayed focused, managed the final minutes and refused to allow the setback to derail the result. Sue’s Angels successfully saw out the remaining time and walked away with all three points.

It was not the team’s best performance, and there will be areas to improve as the season develops. Three Little Birds deserve credit for the problems they caused and for remaining competitive until the final whistle.

For Sue’s Angels, however, the opening day was ultimately about getting points on the board.

Following a historic debut campaign and a two-division rise, the expectations surrounding the club are naturally higher. The performance may not have reached the standards the players have set for themselves, but successful seasons are not built solely on comfortable victories.

They are also shaped by difficult matches when a team must adapt, remain together and find a way to win.

The Angels did exactly that.

Three different formations. Two excellent goals. One determined team performance. Most importantly, three points.

The League Eight campaign is underway.
