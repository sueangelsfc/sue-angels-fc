From finding a way to setting the pace

14 September 2026 · 4 min read · Sue's Angels FC

News From finding a way to setting the pace 14 Sept 2026Sue’s Angels FC

Sue’s Angels sit at the top of League Eight after winning their opening two matches, but the story of their start cannot be explained by the table alone.

The victories arrived in completely different ways.

On the opening day, the Angels defeated Three Little Birds 2–1.

It was a difficult match in which the opposition started strongly and created several opportunities. Munns made eight saves, while the Angels moved through three formations as they attempted to gain control and eventually protect their lead.

Lloyd and Dunkley scored the goals, but Sue’s Angels did not reach their expected level.

The performance left the players with two truths.

They had collected three points in their first League Eight fixture, but they also knew significant improvement would be required.

Seven days later, the response arrived.

Haydons Park were beaten 8–1 as the Angels produced the type of football that defined their strongest performances during the previous campaign.

The pressing was aggressive. Possession was moved with patience and purpose. The attacking players combined effectively, while Haydons Park were prevented from developing any sustained momentum.

Five goals before half-time placed the result beyond serious doubt.

By the final whistle, Owolana and Dunkley had completed hat-tricks. Burnett had converted a penalty, while Brumpton had registered his first competitive goal for the club.

The afternoon also welcomed Jimi back from retirement and introduced Cain and Churchill to competitive football with the Angels.

More than six points

Sue’s Angels have six points from six, but the most encouraging aspect of the opening fortnight has been the variety within the victories.

The first required resilience and tactical flexibility.

The second demonstrated confidence, control and attacking quality.

Successful teams experience matches when their football does not flow. The ability to win those games is important. It becomes even more valuable when the players recognise their shortcomings and produce an immediate response.

That is what Sue’s Angels achieved.

The opening fixtures have also demonstrated the strength of the developing squad.

Summer arrivals have contributed immediately. Established players have maintained their influence. Returning players have provided experience, and two more members of the squad have now made their competitive debuts.

The early League Eight picture

After two fixtures, Sue’s Angels have scored ten goals, conceded two and collected the maximum six points.

Their goal difference of plus eight placed them at the top of League Eight after Gameweek Two.

Several clubs below the Angels have played fewer matches, so it remains far too early to draw conclusions from the standings.

However, the position confirms that Sue’s Angels have adapted quickly following their two-division promotion.

Last season, the Angels won all 18 league fixtures and secured the League Ten title during their first year as a club.

The move into League Eight was always going to demand more. The opposition would be stronger, mistakes would carry a greater cost and the Angels would need to prove themselves again.

The opening-day victory showed that the team could find a way to win when below its best.

The victory over Haydons Park showed what could happen when the performance matched the ambition.

Two matches do not define a season.

They can establish its direction.

Sue’s Angels have made the perfect start. The challenge now is to continue setting the pace.
