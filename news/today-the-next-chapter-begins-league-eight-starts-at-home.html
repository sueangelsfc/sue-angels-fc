Today, the next chapter begins: League Eight starts at home

6 September 2026 · 19 min read · Sue's Angels FC

This morning, the waiting ends.

After a summer of preparation, rebuilding and anticipation, competitive football returns for Sue’s Angels FC today.

At 11:00, Three Little Birds FC will visit The Reeves Sports Club as the Angels begin their 2026/27 Southern Sunday Football League campaign.

It will be the club’s first fixture in League Eight, the opening match of its second competitive season and the next step in a journey that has already created unforgettable memories.

Twelve months ago, Sue’s Angels were preparing to play their first competitive match as a newly formed football club.

There was no previous campaign to look back on. There was no established league record, no reputation and no guarantee of what the group could achieve.

There was simply a collection of players, coaches, friends and volunteers brought together by football and by a shared commitment to honour the memory of Susan Anne Martin.

By the end of that first season, the Angels had made history.

Today, after an extraordinary debut campaign, they begin again.

A first season that will never be forgotten

Sue’s Angels entered League Ten at the beginning of the 2025/26 season with ambition, belief and something greater than football behind the badge.

The team had been assembled, the identity of the club had been established and the purpose was clear. What remained unknown was how quickly everything would come together on the pitch.

The answer arrived almost immediately.

The Angels won their opening league fixture and continued winning until every league match had been completed.

Eighteen matches produced eighteen victories.

The team collected the maximum 54 points, finished as unbeaten League Ten champions and secured promotion with a perfect record.

Played 18. Won 18. Fifty-four points from a possible 54.

Some victories came through dominant attacking displays. Others required patience, discipline and the ability to find a way through difficult moments.

The Angels had to win in different conditions, at different venues and against opponents with different approaches. They had to manage expectations as their winning run grew and respond to the increasing determination of every team that wanted to be the first to take points from them.

As the season progressed, Sue’s Angels went from being an unknown new club to the team everybody wanted to stop.

The pressure increased, but the results continued.

The league title was secured before the final fixture, yet the group refused to allow its standards to fall. Once the championship had been won, the objective became even greater.

The players wanted to complete the entire campaign without dropping a single league point.

They achieved it.

The perfect record was not built in one afternoon. It was created through months of consistency, commitment and collective responsibility.

Different players stepped forward at different moments. Goals came from across the squad, while the defensive unit repeatedly provided the platform for the team to perform with confidence.

The season belonged to everyone who represented the badge.

The numbers behind the achievement

The scale of the Angels’ opening campaign extended far beyond the final League Ten table.

Across all competitive competitions, Sue’s Angels played 33 matches.

They won 29, drew once and lost only three.

The team scored 137 goals and conceded just 25.

Those numbers reflected the balance within the group.

The Angels could attack with pace, create chances in different ways and punish opponents when matches began to open up. At the other end, the team defended with discipline and produced a series of clean sheets throughout the campaign.

The club’s biggest competitive victory was a 12-0 win away to Balham Bteckerz in League Ten.

There were other emphatic results, including nine and ten-goal victories during the closing stages of the league campaign. But the season was not defined only by comfortable scorelines.

There were narrow wins, physical contests and matches in which the Angels had to remain patient.

There were days when the football flowed and others when character mattered more than performance.

Those difficult victories played an equally important role in the championship. They showed that the team could continue finding answers when an opponent disrupted its rhythm or when the match did not unfold as expected.

That resilience will be needed again at the higher level.

A debut season filled with cup memories

The Angels’ first year also included memorable cup journeys.

Alongside the perfect league campaign, Sue’s Angels competed against teams from different levels and tested themselves in unfamiliar environments.

The club progressed through several rounds of cup competition and reached the final of the Dylan Rigobert Trophy.

For a team completing its first season, reaching a final represented another significant achievement.

The final ended in a 3-0 defeat against FC Porto of London. It was a disappointing result on the day, but it did not erase the progress required to reach that stage.

The occasion gave the players experience of preparing for a final, competing under greater pressure and representing the club on another important platform.

It also provided a reminder that progress does not remove the possibility of disappointment.

Success must be earned repeatedly. There will always be another opponent, another challenge and another standard to reach.

The Angels ended their first campaign with a league championship, a perfect record and the experience of competing in a cup final.

Together, those achievements created the foundation for what begins this morning.

More than a trophy

Winning League Ten did not simply add the first piece of silverware to the club’s history.

It earned Sue’s Angels a two-division rise.

The Angels will compete in League Eight throughout the 2026/27 season, moving beyond League Nine and entering a division filled with new opponents and greater challenges.

That rise is recognition of everything the team achieved during its debut campaign.

It is also a demand to improve.

The margins are likely to become smaller. Mistakes will be punished more regularly and matches may remain competitive for longer.

Opponents will arrive with experience, quality and ambitions of their own. There will be moments when the Angels cannot dominate possession or create chances as freely as they did last season.

The team will need to remain composed when games become tight and resilient when momentum moves in the opposite direction.

Last season created belief.

This season will test how strong that belief has become.

The next level begins now

Moving from League Ten to League Eight is an important moment in the club’s development.

It gives Sue’s Angels an opportunity to discover how their football compares against stronger and more established opposition.

The players have earned the right to test themselves at this level.

However, promotion does not guarantee that the next stage will be comfortable.

The title, the perfect record and the goals scored during 2025/26 offer no advantage once the opening whistle is blown this morning.

They provide confidence and experience, but they cannot win a tackle, defend a cross or finish an opportunity.

Everything must be earned again.

The challenge is not to reproduce every scoreline from last season. It is to carry forward the standards that made those results possible.

The work rate.

The discipline.

The togetherness.

The willingness to take responsibility when the match becomes difficult.

Those qualities must travel with the team into League Eight.

Eight opponents stand in the way

Sue’s Angels will face a new group of teams during the 2026/27 campaign.

Barnes Stormers, Bristol City (London) Supporters, Brockwell Violets, Haydons Park, Junction Elite, Pure Football, Three Little Birds and TSM Rovers will provide the opposition across the season.

Some of those clubs will be familiar from cup competitions and previous fixtures outside the league. Others will present completely new challenges.

Every team will bring its own identity.

There will be opponents who look to control possession, teams prepared to defend before attacking quickly, and sides determined to turn matches into physical battles.

The Angels will need to recognise those differences and adapt.

Winning a league requires more than one way of playing.

The team must be able to control matches when the opportunity is there, remain patient when space is limited and compete when the quality of football alone is not enough.

There will be mornings when everything works and others when the performance feels difficult from the opening minutes.

The response in those moments will help determine what Sue’s Angels can achieve in League Eight.

Three Little Birds arrive at The Reeves

The first challenge comes from Three Little Birds FC.

Like Sue’s Angels, this morning’s visitors are entering unfamiliar surroundings and beginning a new chapter.

Three Little Birds competed in the Metropolitan Sunday Football League last season before moving across to the Southern Sunday Football League for 2026/27.

They finished eighth in an eight-team division, recording three victories and two draws from 21 league matches.

Their final position suggests they endured a difficult campaign, particularly during the closing stages of the season.

However, the league table does not tell the complete story of what they are capable of producing.

Three Little Birds demonstrated their attacking threat in several convincing victories.

Their campaign included emphatic 5-1 and 5-0 wins at home, alongside impressive 5-1 and 5-2 victories away from home.

Those performances show that the visitors are capable of scoring heavily when given space, confidence and momentum.

The Angels must ensure they are not allowed to find any of those things easily this morning.

A fresh start for the visitors

Three Little Birds’ move into a new competition offers them the opportunity to reset.

Players may have arrived during the summer. Previous weaknesses may have been addressed and the squad beginning today’s match may look very different from the one that completed last season.

A change of league can create fresh energy around a club.

It removes the weight of previous results and gives every player the opportunity to begin with a clean record.

Three Little Birds will travel to The Reeves knowing that victory over last season’s unbeaten League Ten champions would provide the perfect introduction to their new competition.

That motivation makes them dangerous.

Sue’s Angels cannot approach the fixture believing that last season’s records will determine what happens today.

The two clubs competed in different leagues, faced different opponents and experienced different circumstances.

Neither previous table will be present on the pitch this morning.

At 11:00, the score will be 0-0.

No history and no assumptions

There is no established rivalry between Sue’s Angels and Three Little Birds.

There is no previous meeting to analyse and no direct result that can be used to measure one side against the other.

That uncertainty adds another layer to the opening fixture.

The Angels will not know exactly how Three Little Birds intend to approach the match.

The visitors may press aggressively from the opening whistle. They may choose to defend in a compact shape and attack on the counter. They may look to make the contest physical and prevent the Angels from settling into their rhythm.

Sue’s Angels will need to read the game quickly.

They must recognise the spaces available, understand where the danger is coming from and make the necessary adjustments throughout the match.

That requires communication, concentration and patience.

Opening-day energy can sometimes make teams rush their decisions. The Angels must play with intensity without losing control.

They must respect the opponent without allowing that respect to weaken their confidence.

The objective is to impose their own standards while remaining prepared for whatever challenge Three Little Birds present.

A summer of preparation

The period between seasons has given Sue’s Angels time to prepare for the step into League Eight.

Pre-season has included a varied schedule designed to provide minutes, test combinations and challenge the group against opponents with different qualities.

The results have been mixed.

There have been victories, a draw and defeats.

There have been encouraging performances, alongside difficult periods that exposed areas in which the team needed to improve.

That is the purpose of pre-season.

It is not there to extend an unbeaten record or create the appearance of perfection.

It exists to prepare players for competitive football.

The summer fixtures have allowed members of the squad to rebuild fitness, develop partnerships and gain experience against teams capable of punishing mistakes.

The Angels have experienced matches in which they controlled long periods of play and others in which they were forced to defend, recover and find different solutions.

Those challenges should prove valuable.

A team learns little when every match is comfortable.

It learns much more when the game forces players to communicate, solve problems and remain together when momentum moves against them.

The results will not count towards the League Eight table.

The lessons must.

Preparation must now become performance

This morning is where the experimentation ends.

There are points available.

The intensity will rise, every challenge will carry greater importance and each moment can influence the outcome.

Sue’s Angels must transfer the work completed during the summer into a competitive performance.

The opening stages will be particularly important.

Both sides will arrive with energy and a desire to establish control. The Angels will need to begin sharply, compete for second balls and avoid giving the visitors encouragement through unnecessary mistakes.

Patience will also matter.

The first goal may not arrive immediately. The match may require the team to keep moving the ball, continue creating opportunities and trust the process rather than forcing the final pass.

If Three Little Birds start strongly, the Angels must remain composed.

If Sue’s Angels take control, they must use that control properly.

League football rewards teams capable of managing every phase of a match, not only the moments when they are playing well.

The challenge of the second season

A club’s second season presents a different psychological test from its first.

When Sue’s Angels entered League Ten last year, they were unknown.

There were no achievements for opponents to discuss and no title-winning record for anybody to target.

That has changed.

Sue’s Angels begin 2026/27 as unbeaten champions.

Every opponent will know that the team won all 18 of its league fixtures last season. Every club will see the Angels as a side worth beating.

The badge now carries a reputation that did not exist twelve months ago.

That attention should be treated as a privilege.

It has been earned through performance.

But reputation alone will not protect the Angels when matches become difficult.

The players must continue doing the work that created the reputation in the first place.

Training must remain competitive. Standards must continue to be enforced and every member of the squad must remain accountable to the group.

The championship cannot be allowed to create complacency.

Winning last season does not make the Angels entitled to success this season.

If anything, the standards must become higher.

From champions to challengers

Sue’s Angels will always be the 2025/26 League Ten champions.

That title is part of the club’s history and cannot be taken away.

But the players must enter League Eight as challengers.

This season is not about protecting an unbeaten record that belongs to a different division.

It is about pursuing the next level with the same hunger that existed before the first match in the club’s history.

There should be confidence in what the group has achieved, but there can be no assumption that another perfect campaign will follow.

League Eight will contain difficult mornings.

There will be matches when the Angels are forced to defend for longer than they would like. There will be occasions when chances are missed, decisions go against them or the opposition performs at a high level.

There may be setbacks.

The strongest teams are not those that avoid every difficult moment. They are the teams capable of responding when those moments arrive.

The ability to respond was present throughout the Angels’ first season.

It will become even more important during their second.

Opportunities across the squad

A new campaign brings new opportunities.

Every player begins the season with the chance to influence what comes next.

The individuals who played central roles in last season’s success will want to continue setting the standard. Those who received fewer minutes will have another opportunity to establish themselves.

New additions can bring different qualities and increase competition across the squad.

That internal competition will be important.

A long season cannot be carried by eleven players.

Availability will change. Injuries and suspensions may arrive. Form will rise and fall, and different fixtures will require different solutions.

Every member of the squad must remain ready.

The strength of Sue’s Angels during their first campaign came from the collective. Goals, assists, defensive contributions and important moments arrived from throughout the team.

That same depth will be required in League Eight.

This morning represents the first opportunity for the group to show how it has developed.

The importance of beginning at home

Starting the season at The Reeves Sports Club gives the Angels an opportunity to take their first League Eight step in familiar surroundings.

The Reeves has become the club’s home.

It is where the players, coaches, families, friends and supporters have gathered to share the journey.

It has witnessed victories, goals and the gradual development of a new football club into an unbeaten league champion.

This morning, it will host another important moment.

Home advantage is created by more than familiarity with the pitch.

It comes from the energy around the ground, the encouragement from the touchline and the feeling that the entire club is moving in the same direction.

A strong presence beside the pitch can lift the team during difficult periods and help create momentum when the Angels are on top.

That support will matter throughout the season.

League Eight will test the group in new ways, and the connection between the players and everyone surrounding the club will remain important.

Sue’s Angels have never been built around the eleven players on the pitch alone.

The progress has always been collective.

Still built in her name

Whatever changes on the pitch, the purpose behind Sue’s Angels remains unchanged.

The club was founded in memory of Susan Anne Martin and continues to play for sepsis awareness.

That purpose is not simply a message added to the team.

It is the foundation upon which the club was created.

Every match provides another opportunity to carry Susan’s name forward and encourage more people to understand the warning signs of sepsis.

The football matters.

The results matter.

The ambition to compete, improve and win remains strong.

But the badge represents something deeper than a league position or a final score.

It represents memory, family, friendship and the determination to create something positive that can continue growing.

Promotion provides a bigger platform for that purpose.

As the club moves through the divisions and faces new opponents, more players, supporters and communities will discover what Sue’s Angels represents.

Every fixture allows the message to reach somebody new.

Every person who wears the shirt becomes part of carrying that message.

The team plays in her name.

The players compete for one another.

The club continues for everyone connected to its story.

That will remain true regardless of the division, the opposition or the result.

The standard remains the same

The surroundings will be familiar this morning.

The players will enter the same dressing room, step onto the same home pitch and see many of the same faces beside the touchline.

But almost everything else will feel different.

The league is new.

The opponent is new.

The pressure is different and the challenge is greater.

That is exactly what the Angels earned.

The expectation is not perfection.

It is commitment.

Compete for every ball.

Communicate through every phase of the match.

Support one another when mistakes happen.

Remain disciplined when the game becomes difficult.

Play with confidence when opportunities appear.

Represent the name on the shirt properly from the first whistle until the last.

Those are the standards that created last season’s success.

They must remain at the centre of everything the team does next.

Setting the tone for the season

The opening fixture will not decide where either team finishes in May.

There will be many more matches, challenges and opportunities to follow.

However, the first performance can establish the tone.

Sue’s Angels will want to show from the opening minutes that they are prepared to compete at the higher level.

That does not mean the team must play the perfect match.

It means displaying the behaviours that will be required throughout the campaign.

Intensity without recklessness.

Confidence without complacency.

Ambition without losing discipline.

Togetherness when the match becomes uncomfortable.

Three points would provide the ideal beginning, but the performance must also demonstrate that the team understands the challenge ahead.

The Angels are not entering League Eight to admire the level.

They are entering it to compete.

The first step of a longer journey

One match will never define an entire season.

A victory today would provide the perfect start, but it would still represent only the first step.

A difficult result would not decide what follows either.

A league campaign is built over months.

It requires consistency when energy is high and discipline when the weather changes, availability becomes difficult and the excitement of the opening weeks has faded.

It requires players to remain connected through victories and disappointments.

The Angels demonstrated those qualities last season.

Now they must reproduce them in a stronger division.

Today matters because it provides the first opportunity to collect points and make an impression.

But the club’s ambition extends far beyond one morning.

Sue’s Angels want to prove that the success of their debut season was not an isolated achievement.

The club wants to continue developing, competing and building something capable of lasting.

The League Ten trophy is part of the foundation.

Today, another level begins to be built on top of it.

Today, the next chapter begins

This morning, Sue’s Angels will walk onto the pitch as unbeaten League Ten champions.

They will leave it having completed their first match in League Eight.

Between those two moments will be 90 minutes of competitive football against an opponent beginning a new journey of its own.

Three Little Birds will arrive looking to make an immediate impact in the Southern Sunday Football League.

Sue’s Angels will be determined to establish themselves at the higher level and begin the new season strongly in front of their home support.

Nothing is guaranteed.

That is what makes the opening day special.

The table is empty.

Every team begins on zero points and the possibilities remain open.

Last season has been written into the history of Sue’s Angels FC.

Today, the club begins writing what comes next.

Be there for the beginning

Bring your voice.

Bring your support.

Be there when the next chapter begins.
