The full report.

Sue’s Angels Complete Historic Perfect Season With Final Day Victory

The story ended exactly how everyone connected with Sue’s Angels FC would have hoped. On a sun-blessed final day at The Reeves Sports Club, we dispatched Hillside Elite FC Blues 2-0 to complete an unthinkable feat: eighteen wins from eighteen league matches. Not a single dropped point. Not a single shared spoil. A perfect season in League Ten, the first of its kind since the league’s inception in 2021/22. When the whistle went, the significance of it all settled in properly. This wasn’t just a win. This was history.

The XI that lined up to seal it reflected the blend of consistency and depth we’ve built: Luke Munns in goal, Andrew Allen at left back, Daniel McLane and Jim El Bayati at centre half – with El Bayati wearing the captain’s armband – Dean Knight on the right, and a midfield pairing of Stewart Luwawa and Kafele Brown sitting deep. Elis Brumpton operated centrally ahead of them, while Jon Lloyd and Frazier-Isaías Osunkoya worked the flanks. Alfie James Inman led the line up front. The bench brought Stephen Potter, Jeev Thilaganathan, Samakab Nur, and Michael Brabrook – fresh legs for a day when control of the contest mattered as much as the scoreline itself.

The occasion carried weight beyond our own ambitions. Hillside arrived needing victory to secure promotion, while down the table Brockwell Violets had already finished their season and needed us to do them a favour. The atmosphere crackled with that peculiar tension that comes when promotion, records, and history all converge on ninety minutes of football. Yet we started professionally and controlled large periods of the game, keeping Hillside at arm’s length despite the desperation they brought to their task. The opposition remained organised and competitive throughout – you don’t reach a promotion position by accident – but we were never really in danger.

Daniel McLane broke the deadlock in the twentieth minute, finishing from a corner kick to open the scoring. It was vintage McLane, really. He’s been one of our most dangerous threats from set pieces all season, a menace to defences when the ball swings in from wide, and his instinct to be in the right place at the right moment showed again here. With that early goal, the pressure shifted. Hillside had to chase the game. We could dictate.

The second half saw us put the match beyond doubt when Frazier-Isaías Osunkoya doubled the lead in the 48th minute. The goal came after a superb solo run from Frazier – direct, powerful, exactly the kind of moment that decides matches at this level. Alfie James Inman provided the assist, and with the score at 2-0, the contest was settled. Hillside’s promotion hopes withered further when one of their players received a second yellow card and a red, leaving them chasing the result with ten men. The referee managed the occasion well throughout, keeping the focus on the football despite the pressures and significance surrounding everything.

When the final whistle came, we’d done it. Eighteen wins from eighteen. Fifty-four points – a League Ten record. Ninety goals scored across the league alone. Just eleven conceded. The best defensive record in the division’s history. Brockwell Violets got their promotion alongside us as a bonus, which felt right somehow – they’d done their part. Hillside’s push fell just short, and you couldn’t help but feel for them, but that’s the nature of final days.

This is Sue’s Angels FC’s first season as a club. In thirty-three matches across all competitions, we’ve scored 137 goals and won eighty-eight per cent of our games. We’ve reached a cup final, won our league by a margin that speaks to genuine superiority, and claimed promotion at a canter. We’re the highest-scoring team across the entire Southern Sunday Football League pyramid. History, records, perfection – it’s been an extraordinary debut. And now it’s done. The final chapter of an unforgettable first year written exactly as it should have been.
